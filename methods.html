Methods and limitations

What a record is, and what it cannot support.

Each medium is somebody else's composition, re-encoded against a standard set of exchange reactions so a genome-scale model can read it. Every assumption in that encoding is below, with the field that records it.

Component identity

A component claims a named ingredient is a particular molecule, and for nearly all of them a particular BiGG exchange reaction. Twelve evidence tiers, grouped into six classes ordered by strength, record what decided each claim.

Recorded tierClassComponents

A name match records a string, not a chemical identity: it cannot tell a vitamer group from a vitamer. A cross-reference shown against a component describes the BiGG metabolite that was chosen, not the evidence that chose it.

Where the exchange ids land

A component reaches a BiGG exchange reaction that exists, an id shaped like one that names no BiGG reaction, a fallback id in exchange position, or nothing. The four states partition the components, and only the first is usable by a model.

Neither of the two failing ids is a near miss. EX_cpd15137_e names a real ModelSEED compound that no BiGG model has a reaction for. EX_choles_e is shaped like a BiGG exchange and is not one: BiGG carries cholesterol as choles_c only, with no extracellular form, and the exchange that does exist is EX_chsterol_e. Both are skipped by the copy-to-COBRApy block, and both are dropped in silence by model.medium = {…}, because the reaction is in no model.

What each coverage fraction divides by

Three fields read as coverage and divide by different denominators. All ship on every record; this site displays the first.

FieldDivides byStatus
pct_covered_source The ingredients the cited source states. Derived components are in neither the numerator nor the denominator. Displayed here. Some records show it as a ceiling, marked "at most".
pct_sourced_components_with_bigg_id The components the record already carries, not the source's ingredient list, so it is not a coverage measure. On every record; null where the source states no ingredient list. Earlier exports name it pct_covered_observed.
pct_covered Every component the record ends up with, derived ones included, so it is near 100% by construction. Deprecated. Kept for compatibility; not displayed.

Components the source never stated

A derived component has one of three origins, none a measurement: a complex ingredient decomposed against a standard composition reference ("beef extract" expanded into amino acids and vitamins), a hydrolysate approximated (peptone, tryptone, yeast extract), or a mineral and oxygen base injected so a model is not starved of an element the source took for granted. They are marked with a dashed rule wherever they appear, excluded from every source-coverage figure, and listed separately in the copy-to-COBRApy block.

Bounds are not measured uptake rates

Almost every lower bound here is a presence placeholder: it encodes "this is in the medium", not "this is taken up at this rate". Treat a bound as a switch and set your own numbers before interpreting a flux.

An amount, where one exists, is published verbatim in quantity with its basis. A mass per 100 g of food is not a concentration, and the conversion needs a volume nobody measured, so concentration_mM is null wherever it is not derivable and the component names what blocks it.

Two media, one model input

What reaches a solver is the set of (exchange reaction, lower bound, upper bound) triples, not the name, the source or the citation. By that measure a large fraction of this library is degenerate: presence placeholders collapse different amounts onto one constraint set, and many compositions differ only in something a record does not encode, such as agar or pH.

Measuring the library.

Records are not deduplicated by model input: which paper a composition came from is a real difference even when the constraint set is not. Adding each component's concentration leaves most of them indistinguishable. Each record sheet names the other media in its group, and the groups are published in full.

Oxygen regime

The oxygen regime decides whether EX_o2_e is open or closed. It has four states, and unknown is one of them: unknown is a state of the field, not a synonym for anaerobic. The regime is recorded only where a source or a curator asserted one, so most of this library is unknown. A medium with no stated regime still ships with EX_o2_e open, by the same convention that supplies the mineral base; that oxygen row is marked derived on every record that carries it and is excluded from every source-coverage figure. The convention is not a finding, and it is published separately from the regime so the two can never be read as one number.

Base-medium families

A family groups the variants of one base medium. It is a naming grouping, not a claim that the variants are interchangeable: most assignments come from the medium's name, and a record carries a corroboration score against the family scaffold where one could be computed. The method is in family.method.

Reproducibility

The per-record files are the authoritative copy of the library and cannot be regenerated from their original inputs: a fresh extraction would file different compositions under the same citations. Corrections are applied forward, as transform stages run in a recorded order, and the chain is re-run from a committed baseline and compared against the corpus that ships.

The limits

  1. Component identities mostly rest on a name string.
  2. A large minority of component records are derived and are not in the cited source.
  3. Bounds are presence placeholders, not measured uptake rates.
  4. Records are not deduplicated by their model input.
  5. Not every component reaches a BiGG exchange, and an id shaped like one is not always one.
  6. Not every component carries a cross-reference.
  7. The oxygen regime is not recorded for every medium.
  8. Food records are a population-average nutrient analysis, not a laboratory formulation; their amounts are per 100 g and are not concentrations.
  9. A family assignment is usually a name match.
  10. The corpus is a frozen snapshot and cannot be regenerated from its raw upstream inputs.

How to cite

Cite the primary publication in the record's provenance.citation, and name this resource and its release stamp where the compilation itself was load-bearing.
