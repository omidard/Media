Methods and limitations

What a record here is, and what it is not.

This resource is a compilation. Every medium is somebody else's formulation, re-encoded against a standard set of exchange reactions so a genome-scale model can use it. That re-encoding introduces assumptions, and this page states them, with counts, so you can decide what to trust.

How a component's identity was decided

Every component in this library is a claim that a named ingredient is a particular molecule with a particular BiGG exchange reaction. The pipeline records how each of those claims was reached, and groups the twelve recorded evidence tiers into six classes. The classes are ordered by the strength of the evidence.

The most important thing on this page.

An earlier release of this resource labelled 42% of components exact and rendered that word in bold green as its trust signal. Almost all of those were a hard-coded English-label lookup, not a chemical identity check. The word has been withdrawn. What is published now is what actually decided the identity, and for most components that is a name string.

Recorded tierClassComponents

A name match is a fallback, not a confirmation. It cannot detect that a source's "Cystine" is a different molecule from L-cysteine, that a "MUFA 18:1" measurement pooled every 18:1 isomer, or that "Vitamin B-6" is a vitamer group rather than pyridoxine. Where the pipeline collapsed a class onto one representative molecule it says so, in the class or assertion tier, rather than presenting the assumption as a measurement.

What coverage means, and what it used to mean

Three different fractions can be called "coverage" and they do not agree. This resource publishes all three and names each one.

FieldWhat it divides byStatus
pct_covered_source The ingredients the cited source itself states. Pipeline-derived components are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. The number this site displays.
pct_covered_observed The mapped share of the components the source actually stated. Published on each record. Null for the records that state nothing.
pct_covered Every component the record ended up with, including the ones the pipeline invented. That ratio is necessarily near 100% and carries no information. Deprecated. Kept in the data because it was published, never rendered here as a quality signal.
Source coverage is sometimes a ceiling, not a point estimate.

Some records count the source ingredients that were replaced by derived components as a floor, so the true denominator is at least that large and the percentage is at most the one shown. Those records say "at most" beside the number.

Components the source never stated

A large minority of the component records in this library were supplied by the pipeline rather than read from a source. There are three reasons this happens, and none of them is a measurement:

  • Complex-ingredient decomposition. A recipe says "beef extract"; a standard composition reference is used to expand that into amino acids, nucleotides, vitamins and minerals. The recipe never listed those molecules.
  • Hydrolysate approximation. The same idea for peptones, tryptone, yeast extract and casein hydrolysates, which are undefined mixtures by construction.
  • Injected mineral and oxygen base. Water, protons, phosphate, sulfate, ammonium and trace metals are added so a model is not starved of an element the source took for granted. This is a modelling convention, not the recipe.
They are kept, never deleted.

Removing them would make thousands of media infeasible in flux balance analysis, and that failure would be blamed on the model. So they ship, they carry a dashed marker everywhere they appear, they are excluded from every source-coverage number, and the copy-to-COBRApy block puts them in their own commented section so you can delete them yourself.

Bounds are not measured uptake rates

Almost every lower bound in this library is a presence placeholder: it encodes "this is in the medium", not "this is taken up at this rate". Treat a bound as a switch, and set your own numbers before interpreting a flux.

Where an amount exists it is published verbatim with its basis: a mass per 100 g of food is not a concentration, and converting one into the other requires a volume of medium that nobody measured. This resource does not perform that conversion, and it does not present a mass fraction in the same column as a molar concentration.

Oxygen regime

The oxygen regime decides whether you open or close EX_o2_e, which is the single most consequential bound in an FBA medium. It has four states here, and one of them is "we do not know".

An earlier release displayed the unknowns as "anaerobic".

A missing value fell through a boolean test into the anaerobic branch, and the oxygen filter returned those records as confirmed anaerobes. Unknown is now its own state, in the table, in the filter and on the record. A facultative regime is also shown as facultative and is not evidence that the organism is an aerobe.

Base-medium families

A family groups the variants of one base medium so that "LB" is one thing with variants rather than dozens of unrelated rows. How each family was decided is recorded on the record and shown in the browser.

A family is a naming grouping. It is not a claim that the variants are interchangeable.

Most assignments are made from the medium's name. Where the pipeline could also check the record's composition against the family's canonical scaffold it publishes that corroboration score, and where it could not it publishes the reason instead of a guess. Two records in one family can differ by a carbon source, by strength, by pH, or by being broth against agar. The families view shows what differs rather than averaging it away.

Licensing is per source, not per repository

There is no blanket data licence here. Each record carries the terms of the source it came from. An earlier release placed the whole of data/ under CC BY 4.0, which grants commercial use; a substantial minority of the upstream sources grant no such right. Nothing was removed to fix that. Every record ships, labelled, and the browser can filter for the commercially usable subset.

The full schedule, with the verbatim terms and the date each was read, is in LICENSE; the attribution notices are in NOTICE. Code in this repository is MIT. Whatever the licence, cite the primary publication in each record's provenance.citation: this compilation is not the source of the formulation.

Reproducibility, stated honestly

The original builders cannot be re-run.

Nineteen generator scripts hard-coded a temporary scratchpad directory that has since been deleted, and the raw upstream payloads were never committed. For most of this library data/media/*.json is the only surviving copy. That is a limitation of this resource and it is not recoverable by re-running anything.

Corrections are therefore made as transform stages: a stage reads the corpus and writes a corrected corpus, and the chain of stages, run in a recorded order, is the pipeline. No correction is hand-edited into the data. The stage that builds everything this browser reads is tools/stages/52_web_payload.py, and it asserts its own numbers against the corpus before the payload is written.

Public upstream sources (USDA, MediaDive, BiGG, MetaNetX) have been re-acquired and checksummed into data/_sources/, so they can be compared against. The literature-derived records came from extraction batches that no longer exist; re-running that miner would file different compositions under the same citations, so it is not run.

Records that were assessed and withdrawn

A verification pass read source publications and rejected media it could not support. Those records are not served, and until now nothing on the site said so. They are listed here with the reason, because a rejection is a result.

This count has no denominator.

The verification pass recorded what it rejected but not how many records it assessed, so the number below is a count and cannot honestly be turned into a rate.

The limits, in one list

  1. Most component identities rest on a name string, not on chemistry. A name match cannot distinguish a vitamer group from a vitamer, or a fatty-acid class from an isomer.
  2. About a third of all component records were supplied by this pipeline and are not in the cited source. They are an approximation of an undefined mixture, or a modelling convention.
  3. Bounds are presence placeholders, with a small minority of source-stated concentrations. No bound in this library is a measured uptake rate.
  4. Food records are built from a population-average nutrient analysis, not from a laboratory formulation. Their amounts are per 100 g of food; they are not a concentration and this resource does not convert them into one.
  5. Cross-references shown against a component describe the BiGG metabolite that was chosen. They were not used to choose it, so they cannot contradict it and they are not independent evidence.
  6. The corpus is a frozen snapshot whose original generators are gone, so it cannot be rebuilt from its raw inputs. Corrections are made forward, as stages.
  7. Records are not deduplicated by composition. Some media are compositionally identical to others; where that is known the record says so.
  8. A family assignment is usually a name match. It does not assert that the variants in a family are interchangeable.
  9. The oxygen regime is unknown for some records. Unknown is not anaerobic.
  10. The licence is per source. A substantial minority of records may not be used commercially, and this resource cannot grant rights it does not hold.

How to cite

Cite the primary publication recorded in the record's provenance.citation, and, if the compilation itself was load-bearing for your result, name this resource and the release stamp shown at the top of this page. A record here is an encoding of somebody else's formulation, and the encoding is where the assumptions live.
